How the three options differ

A - Rung pulses is the metronome: the pawn snaps onto the rails, then progress advances in four discrete pulses (dwell, pull, dwell) with an alternating lateral rock and lean - stairs glide, ladders tick. B - Quick slide gives ladders a gameplay personality instead: going down is a fast fireman slide with a stretch and a landing squash, going up a rapid continuous scramble with high-frequency micro-bobs; total latency roughly half of A, making ladders the cheap-but-single-file link. C - Peek and drop spends extra ticks on character: an anticipation beat first (the pawn leans over the shaft and checks), then two big committed pulls; arrivals pop out head-first with an overshoot bounce before climbing clear. All three share the scale grammar (0.32 below the viewed band, 1.14 + fade above), because a ladder is still a hole in the floor.

Implementation guardrail (run #297)

Whatever option wins, the mount + climb + hold latency must not gate the carry (ABStairAnim.ReadyToCarry on TryConsumeArrival broke movement once). Carry instantly, immobilize briefly on the far side, ghost the origin-side clip, then play the emerge clip. The pulses, rock, squash and lean all live in the pawn draw matrix, same as the stairs shimmy.
