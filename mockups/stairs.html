How the three options differ

A - Depth glide is the cinematic one: one smooth eased slide into the well with the shipped shimmy (amplitude 0.10 cells, rate 17), the pawn's scale encoding altitude - shrink to 0.32 going below the viewed band (today's MinScale), grow to 1.14 and fade going above, and arrivals mirror it (from below grows, from above settles). B - Tread steps trades smoothness for literalism: progress advances in five discrete hops with a bob and an alternating lean, like the pawn is actually taking the treads; same scale grammar, chunkier read, slightly longer defaults. C - Doorway slip is the minimal, most vanilla-feeling one: the pawn never changes size - it keeps its ordinary walk cycle and is progressively clipped by the well mouth, as if walking behind a wall edge, with a dust puff and a brightness shift as the only depth cues. C is also the cheapest in game: no matrix scaling, just draw-order/clip against the stair sprite plus the existing hide machinery.

Implementation guardrail (run #297)

Whatever option wins, the entry + hold latency must not gate the carry: ABStairAnim.ReadyToCarry on TryConsumeArrival is the exact thing that broke cross-level movement once. Proposed mechanism instead: the carry stays instant; the arrived pawn is briefly immobilized on the far side (stagger-style, self-clearing) while a visual-only ghost plays the entry clip at the origin mouth, then the arrival clip plays. Felt result is identical to this mockup; the pather never waits on a cosmetic.
