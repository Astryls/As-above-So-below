  • RimWorld 1.6 keeps all Core/DLC terrain art inside Unity asset bundles — Data/Core/ has no Textures/ folder — and Modmixer ships no image compositor. So I cannot open vanilla soil/grass/rock PNGs directly.

Waterfalls & lakes — your spec, costed

applies to any option above

Your spec: at map gen, a river flowing away from a mountain is raised to the upper level instead of tunnelling; the lower level is filled to the mountain perimeter; at the band edge the water falls with FX/steam + audio and continues as a normal river on the surface. Lakes never spawn near an upper-level edge.

How it would actually be built (feasible route):

  • Post-process, don't reimplement. Vanilla river generation is one of the gnarlier gen systems. The safe route is to let it run, then read the generated river cells and decide per-cell: raise to sky band, or keep on the surface. Reimplementing river noise would be a much bigger, riskier job.
  • The fall lip is where a raised (sky) water cell borders a see-through drop cell. That's a cheap adjacency test — the same shape as the existing drop-off ring.
  • FX: mist/steam via vanilla flecks, emitted per fall lip cell, plus a looping water sustainer positioned at the base. Both are standard, but flecks are per-frame draws — they need a hard cap and a distance/zoom gate.
  • Lakes away from edges is genuinely easy: a distance-from-mass-edge test at gen time, reject water placement within N cells. No runtime cost.

Two honest risks I'd want a spike for before committing:

  • Below-view water. Surface water seen from the sky band goes through our see-below renderer. Water shaders read per-frame globals and the water-depth pass runs through its own subcamera — we have a documented case of that rendering as a black slab when drawn out of context. Same-map banding is much more favourable than the cross-map case, but it is unproven. If it fails, surface rivers viewed from above need special handling.
  • Raising water on the sky band means water sitting on a level whose "ground" is our rock field, next to a cliff. Flow/depth shading and the shoreline fade may need their own treatment so it doesn't read as a floating blue rectangle.

My honest recommendation: A + B first, then D, then E.

A is nearly built already and removes the remaining seams. B is the highest payoff per unit of effort on this entire list — a real cliff face is what makes an upper level look purposeful, and it is pure bake-time geometry at a safe render queue, so it cannot regress sprites the way the reverted cover did. D is what makes plateaus worth building on, but it is the only option with a genuine per-frame cost, so it wants a density budget and a measured run. E is the showpiece and also the riskiest — I'd want a small spike on below-view water before promising the full river-raise. C I'd park: it multiplies perimeter (and therefore outline risk) for a payoff that B mostly delivers on its own.
